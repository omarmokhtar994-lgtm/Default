## Q1. How do the implicit break/shift models (Bechtold & Jacobs 1990, Thompson 1995, Aykin 1996/2000, Brusco & Jacobs, Topaloglu, Rekik/Cordeau/Soumis 2004–2010) aggregate breaks, when are they exact, and how much faster are they than explicit models?

### Takeaway
Implicit models replace one variable per (shift, break-placement) combination with separate shift variables and break variables, linked by "forward/backward" constraints. These models match the explicit Dantzig set-covering model in coverage, and their LP relaxations are equivalent to it. They shrink the models from hundreds of millions of variables to thousands (under 1,500 in Brusco & Jacobs 2000). B&J's original version is exact only without "extraordinary overlap" of break windows or start bands. Later work removed that restriction (Rekik et al. 2004 via Benders; the 2007 generalized B&J model) and added multiple breaks and work-stretch (min/max spacing) restrictions (Rekik et al. 2010). CPLEX then solves environments with billions of implicit shifts to optimality.

### Inferences
- **How aggregation works [background, unverified in detail]:** there is one integer variable x_s per shift type/start, and one integer variable y_{b,t} per (break type/window b, break start t). Forward constraints say breaks placed in the first k periods of a window can't exceed shifts whose window is among the first k. Backward constraints do the same from the other end. Total breaks per window equal the number of shifts using it. Coverage at t = Σ shifts working at t − Σ breaks in progress at t. The model is exact in the sense that any feasible (x,y) can be decomposed into individual shift-with-break assignments (Hall's-theorem argument) when there is no extraordinary overlap. Rekik 2004 and the 2007 generalization handle overlap.
- **Mapping to the target scheduler:** a 9 h shift with 15+30+15 min breaks is three break "types", each with its own window. Min/max spacing between breaks maps onto the Rekik 2010 work-stretch restriction. A **cap on concurrent breaks** is linear in the aggregated break variables (Σ breaks in progress at t ≤ cap), so it adds nothing to the implicit model. Coverage after shrinkage just scales the requirement vector.
- Implicit models are **anonymous**. They yield *how many* shifts start when and *how many* breaks start when. A disaggregation step is needed for named associates (see Q5). Kiermaier et al.'s two-stage split (tours first, breaks second) is a pragmatic alternative when per-person rules make a single implicit model awkward.

## Q2. What do the grammar/automaton-based models (Côté, Gendron, Quimper, Rousseau; Restrepo, Gendron, Rousseau 2016–2018) report on instance sizes and gaps?

### Takeaway
Grammar and automaton models compile the rules for building a legal shift (activities, breaks, min/max spells) into a network-flow or and/or-graph IP. They are either implicit (anonymous) or used as the pricing problem in branch-and-price for **personalized** (named, heterogeneous) employees. Reported sizes: up to 10 activities on a one-day horizon (Côté et al. 2011). One-week tours with up to 5 activities solve to **gaps under 1%** by branch-and-price (Restrepo et al. 2016). Weekly instances with up to 10 activities go through Benders plus column generation (Restrepo et al. 2018), which beats the 2016 branch-and-price.

### Inferences
- For the target scheduler the multi-activity dimension is minimal: "work" plus 3 breaks, plus languages as skills rather than activities. A **regular language/DFA per day** would capture the shift + break rules cheaply: 36 fifteen-minute slots, breaks of 1/2/1 slots in windows, min/max work stretch between them. The weekly rules (5 days on, 2 consecutive off, at most 2 distinct shift types, 12 h rest) are a small **resource-constrained DP** over 7 days. That is the Restrepo-2016 "daily columns + tour assembly" or Shariat-Huang "CFG + RCSP" structure.
- Grammar/B&P approaches are the literature's main route to **named, heterogeneous** employees with exact bounds. The published sizes (one week, up to 5–10 activities) are near a mid-size contact center. No paper found here reports explicit employee counts, so scalability to hundreds of agents is unverified (see Gaps).

- No head-to-head reported between grammar B&P and implicit (forward/backward) models on *tour* problems with named employees. The only comparison found is Dahmen et al. 2018 for anonymous shifts (Q3).

## Q3. What is the state of break scheduling specifically (Beer/Gärtner/Musliu/Schafhauser/Slany; Di Gaspero et al.; Dahmen/Rekik/Soumis; Widl & Musliu)?

### Takeaway
There are two lines of work. (a) The **TU Wien / Di Gaspero line** schedules breaks for a *given* shift plan (or co-designs shifts and breaks) with local search: min-conflicts, tabu search, simulated annealing, memetic algorithms and hybrid LS-CP. It uses public benchmarks of 30 instances (20 real, 10 random), with call-center and supervisory applications. Widl & Musliu's improved memetic algorithm set the best known bounds on 28/30. (b) The **Montréal line** (Rekik, Dahmen, Soumis) integrates break placement exactly inside implicit or heuristic multi-activity models: optimal via CPLEX for up to 2.97 billion explicit shifts, and a tabu search + B&B hybrid for heterogeneous multi-day problems.

### Inferences
- The benchmark break-scheduling problem (fixed shifts; place breaks in windows with min/max distances between breaks and min/max work-period lengths; minimize under/over-coverage) is almost exactly the target scheduler's break subproblem. The one addition is a concurrent-break cap, which is easy to add as a hard constraint.
- Two practical designs follow:
  1. Two-stage, as in Kiermaier et al. 2020: tours first, then a break-placement MIP or implicit BAP per day.
  2. Single-stage local search, as in Di Gaspero 2013 SA, with a composite neighborhood over {day off moves, shift-start moves, break moves}. Widl & Musliu's finding that a **time-period-based representation** beats shift-based representation is a useful design hint for a metaheuristic.
- For a per-day break MIP with fixed shifts, implicit break variables (B&J/Aykin-style counts per start) plus a concurrent cap give a small, tight model. Individual break times are then recovered by matching (see Q1 inference).

## Q4. How do branch-and-price / column generation tour-scheduling methods (Mehrotra et al., Brunner & Bard, Brusco & Jacobs, Dantzig, Bard) work, and how do they close integrality gaps and round?

### Takeaway
The Dantzig (1954) set-covering model is the reference. B&P solves it implicitly by generating shift/tour columns through pricing. Mehrotra, Murphy & Trick (2000) is the canonical shift-level B&P with multiple breaks and break windows, tested on 365 instances with 1,728–86,400 shift variations. Brunner & Bard (2013) apply B&P to weekly tours with lunch breaks and flexible days off on USPS data. Gaps are closed with specialized branching rules (Mehrotra), or reported as under 1% (Restrepo 2016). Integer Benders cuts give exactness in Benders+CG (Restrepo 2018).

### Cited Findings
- **Mehrotra, A., Murphy, K.E. & Trick, M.A. (2000). "Optimal shift scheduling: A branch-and-price approach." *Naval Research Logistics* 47(3):185–200.** B&P for staff scheduling with **multiple rest breaks, a meal break and break windows**. **Specialized branching rules** solve the set-covering formulation implicitly by column generation. Tested on **365 problems with 1,728 to 86,400 shift variations and 20 demand patterns**. Against an alternative method it showed significant improvements in CPU time and in the number of instances solved to optimality. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1520-6750(200004)47:3%3C185::AID-NAV1%3E3.0.CO;2-7); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/navres/v47y2000i3p185-200.html); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Optimal-shift-scheduling:-A-branch-and-price-Mehrotra-Murphy/39469d982f975177dda7ee09f05810eb84e5f3a3)
- **Brunner, J.O. & Bard, J.F. (2013). "Flexible weekly tour scheduling for postal service workers using a branch and price." *Journal of Scheduling* 16:129–149.** MILP solved by B&P for mail-processing staff under union and labor rules. ### Inferences
- **[background, unverified]** Common integerization practice in this literature: branch on the aggregated number of shifts starting at period t (Mehrotra-style, "Ryan-Foster"-like for identical employees). For personalized problems, branch on employee–shift–day assignments. Diving/rounding heuristics (fix columns with value ≥ θ, re-solve) produce incumbents. The ≤1% gaps suggest set-covering LP bounds are tight when coverage enters as shortage/overage slacks.
- For the target scheduler, a **per-associate pricing problem** (weekly DP with states day × last-shift-end × set of ≤2 shift types used, and 12 h rest checks) is cheap, since there are only 7 days and a few dozen shift starts. The coverage duals then drive both shift and break placement. The concurrent-break cap and language-coverage rows are just more master rows with duals.

## Q5. Is there literature on threshold objectives (maximizing the number of intervals that meet a target / "service level met") rather than shortage minimization, and how weak is their LP relaxation?

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed tour/shift-scheduling paper was found whose objective is **counting intervals that meet a coverage threshold**. The closest literature uses service-level (SL) targets in one of three ways:
1. **Per-period SL constraints** enforced via simulation plus cutting planes (Atlason/Epelman/Henderson; Avramidis et al.; Ingolfsson et al.; Cezik & L'Ecuyer).
2. A **global (daily) SL** objective or constraint, which is multimodular so local search is globally optimal (Koole & van der Sluis 2003).
3. Shortage/overage minimization with **convex penalties** (e.g., quadratic overstaffing in the 2026 SA paper).

The LP weakness of step/indicator objectives is not treated explicitly in any source found. It is an inference from general MIP theory.

### Cited Findings
- **Koole, G. & van der Sluis, E. (2003). "Optimal shift scheduling with a global service level constraint." *IIE Transactions* 35(11):1049–1055.** Call-center shift scheduling with an **overall (day-level) SL objective**. The problem is proven **multimodular**, so a **local search terminates at a global optimum**. Tested on real call-center data. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07408170304398); [author PDF](https://www.math.vu.nl/~koole/publications/2003iie/art.pdf)
- **Atlason, J., Epelman, M.A. & Henderson, S.G. (2004), *Annals of OR* ("Call center staffing with simulation and cutting plane methods"), and (2008), *Management Science* ("Optimizing call center staffing using simulation and analytic center cutting-plane methods").** These minimize staffing cost subject to SL in multiple periods, where staffing in one period affects SL in later periods. They solve a sample-average approximation, estimate SL by simulation with common random numbers, and add cuts. Convergence holds when the SL functions are **discrete pseudoconcave**. This mostly beats queueing-based heuristics. — [INFORMS 2008](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1070.0774); [Annals OR 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019095.91642.bb); [Cornell PDF](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/shane/pubs/SACCPM.pdf)
- **Avramidis, A.N., Chan, W., Gendreau, M., L'Ecuyer, P. & Pisacane, O. (2010). "Optimizing daily agent scheduling in a multiskill call center." *EJOR* 200(3):822–832.** Minimizes agent cost under expected-SL constraints **per call type, per period, and aggregated**, using simulation plus IP/LP with cut generation. It beat all earlier methods, and the standard **two-step (staffing then scheduling) approach can be highly suboptimal**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221709000332); [PolyPublie](https://publications.polymtl.ca/55018/)
- **Ingolfsson, A., Campello, F., Wu, X. & Cabral, E. (2010). "Combining integer programming and the randomization method to schedule employees." *EJOR* 202(1):153–163.** Low-cost shift schedules whose **time-varying SL always stays above a minimum**. It alternates between an evaluator (transient SL via the randomization method, flagging infeasible intervals) and an IP generator that adds constraints for each infeasible interval. The result is a good feasible solution plus a lower bound, without an optimality guarantee. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221709003014); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2275004)
- Cezik & L'Ecuyer (2008), "Staffing Multiskill Call Centers via Linear Programming and Simulation," *Management Science* 54(2):310–323. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v54y2008i2p310-323.html)
- Bhulai, Koole & Pot (2008), "Simple Methods for Shift Scheduling in Multiskill Call Centers" (*MSOM*). — [author PDF](https://www.math.vu.nl/~sbhulai/publications/msom2007a.pdf)
- A stochastic programming model for scheduling call centers with **global SLAs** (Robbins & Harrison, EJOR 2010, **[background, unverified]** attribution). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222419551_A_stochastic_programming_model_for_scheduling_call_centers_with_global_Service_Level_Agreements)
- The 2026 multi-neighborhood SA uses a **quadratic cost for overstaffing**, a convex surrogate rather than a threshold. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-026-00886-z)

### Inferences
- **LP weakness (inference, not sourced):** "maximize #intervals with coverage ≥ target" needs z_t ∈ {0,1} with coverage_t ≥ target_t · z_t (or a big-M form). In the LP relaxation z_t = coverage_t/target_t gives partial credit, so the relaxation behaves like a linear shortage objective with weight 1/target_t. The bound is loose, and branching must fight many near-fractional z_t. That is a classic reason to prefer:
  - (a) a **piecewise-linear convex shortage penalty**, with a steep segment below the **floor** and a moderate one between floor and **target**. This keeps LP tightness and is what shortage-minimization implicit/B&P models already support.
  - (b) hard floor constraints plus a soft target.
  - (c) a global-SL formulation where concavity/multimodularity (Koole & van der Sluis) lets local search be exact.
- Avramidis et al. show that separating staffing from scheduling can be highly suboptimal. That argues for putting the coverage thresholds directly into the joint tour/shift/break model rather than pre-computing requirements and fitting shifts afterwards.
- **Language minimum coverage** maps to multi-skill per-period constraints (the Avramidis/Cezik & L'Ecuyer style of per-call-type constraints, simplified to headcount floors).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source found that explicitly studies "count of intervals meeting target" (indicator/step) objectives in personnel scheduling, or quantifies their LP gap. This appears to be a gap in the literature, or it was not surfaced under the search constraints.

## Q6. Which approaches handle named heterogeneous employees rather than anonymous tours, and how is disaggregation (assigning tours to people) done?

### Takeaway
Implicit models (B&J, Aykin, Thompson, Brusco & Jacobs, Rekik et al., Dahmen 2018) and Mehrotra-style B&P produce **anonymous** shifts/tours and need a **disaggregation** step. The personalized line (Côté 2013; Restrepo 2016/2018; Dahmen 2015 heterogeneous and the two-stage personalized approach; Shariat & Huang 2024) builds schedules per employee directly, usually via per-employee pricing or construction. For disaggregation, Goodale & Thompson (2004) found that a simple rule works well: assign people in descending productivity/cost ratio.

### Cited Findings
- **"A Comparison of Heuristics for Assigning Individual Employees to Labor Tour Schedules," *Annals of OR* 128:47–63 (2004).** Employees differ in cost and productivity. Considering productivity when assigning people to tours increased profitability. A simple rule, **assign individuals in descending order of productivity-to-cost ratio**, was fast and effective across many service scenarios. Earlier tour literature assumed identical employees. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019098.97205.cc); [Cornell eCommons PDF](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/76f2206b-6466-4fe7-a822-daa6c6db4047/content) (authors **[background, unverified]**: Goodale & Thompson)
- Personalized (named) approaches:
  - Côté, Gendron & Rousseau 2013 (employees with qualifications, preferences, availabilities) — [INFORMS MS page as surfaced](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1100.1264)
  - Restrepo et al. 2016 — [CIRRELT](https://www.cirrelt.ca/documentstravail/cirrelt-2015-03.pdf)
  - Restrepo et al. 2018 — [C&OR](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054818300145)
  - Dahmen, Rekik & Soumis 2015 heterogeneous workforce — [J Sched](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-014-0383-z)
  - Two-stage personalized multi-department — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335011683_A_two-stage_solution_approach_for_personalized_multi-department_multi-day_shift_scheduling)
  - Shariat & Huang 2024 heterogeneous employees — [J Heuristics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10732-024-09527-0)
- **Anonymous models:**
  - Dahmen et al. 2018 explicitly builds "anonymous multi-activity shifts" — [J Sched](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-017-0544-y)
  - The 2026 SA assumes a homogeneous workforce — [J Sched](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-026-00886-z)
  - Brusco & Jacobs 2000 is an implicit anonymous tour model — [INFORMS](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.46.12.1630.12074)
- Topaloglu & Ozkarahan (2004), an implicit goal-programming tour model that considers **employee work preferences**, is a hybrid between anonymous and personalized. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019102.68222.df)
- Survey context: **Van den Bergh, J., Beliën, J., De Bruecker, P., Demeulemeester, E. & De Boeck, L. (2013). "Personnel scheduling: A literature review." *EJOR* 226(3):367–385** (reviews 300+ papers). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221712008776); [KU Leuven Lirias](https://lirias.kuleuven.be/retrieve/231816). A "Survey, Categorization, and Comparison of Recent Tour Scheduling Literature" appears in *Annals of OR* 128 (2004). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019088.98647.e2)

### Inferences
- For a weekly scheduler of **named** associates with personal rules (2 consecutive days off, ≤2 distinct shift types, 12 h rest, languages), the literature suggests three options:
  1. **(Exact-ish)** Branch-and-price with a per-associate weekly pricing DP/automaton, where coverage/floor/language/concurrent-break rows sit in the master. This is the Restrepo 2016 / Côté 2013 pattern, with <1% gaps reported at one-week, ≤5-activity scale.
  2. **(Two-stage)** An implicit anonymous tour + break model (Rekik/Brusco-Jacobs/Kiermaier) for staffing shape, then **disaggregation**. Disaggregation is an assignment/matching MIP from anonymous tours to named associates, respecting personal feasibility (languages, rest, shift-type diversity). Productivity/cost ordering is a simple baseline (Goodale & Thompson 2004). Per-person constraints such as "≤2 distinct shift types" and "12 h rest across days" are not visible to anonymous implicit models, so the stage-2 matching may be infeasible or lossy. This is the main risk of the two-stage route.
  3. **(Heuristic)** LNS/SA over named schedules, with CFG/RCSP or DP repair (Shariat & Huang 2024; Quimper & Rousseau 2010; the 2026 SA), using implicit/LP bounds only for quality assessment.
- Language minimum coverage is naturally per-person (a skill attribute), which favors options (1) and (3) over pure anonymous models.

### Gaps
- Ernst, Jiang, Krishnamoorthy & Sier (2004), "Staff scheduling and rostering: A review of applications, methods and models," *EJOR* 153(1):3–27, was not searched (tool budget), so it is **[background, unverified]**.
- No source found gives quantitative loss from disaggregating anonymous tours to named employees with per-person rules like ≤2 distinct shift types. This matters for choosing between options (1) and (2) above.
