# Matheuristics and Decomposition Methods for Large Personnel (Shift/Tour/Break) Scheduling with Coverage Objectives

Scope note for the report writer: this session's network policy blocked full-text fetches from nearly every academic host (arXiv, Semantic Scholar API, patatconference.org, numdam, strathprints, ojs.aaai.org, gerad.ca, TU Wien, utwente personal pages). Every "Cited Findings" bullet below comes from search-engine result pages and abstract-level summaries. Few quantitative tables could be checked. Items that come from my background knowledge and were **not** re-verified here appear only under "Inferences" or "Gaps" and are marked "(background, unverified)".

Problem framing used throughout: Stage 1 (the skeleton) decides which associate works which days and at which shift start. Stage 2 places breaks day by day on the fixed skeleton and is already near-optimal. The skeleton covers 100% of intervals before breaks, but after breaks it loses up to 10-20% of target intervals on hard instances. The gap is therefore caused by a Stage-1 model that ignores Stage-2 break capacity. This is the classic "shift-then-break" sequential-decomposition failure mode that the literature below addresses.

## Q1. Fix-and-optimize / relax-and-fix / fix-and-relax for rostering (neighborhoods by day, employee, shift type)

### Takeaway
MIP-based fix-and-optimize and VNS matheuristics, which re-optimize a sub-MIP over a subset of employees, days or classes while everything else stays fixed, are well-established winners over monolithic solves on nurse rostering and timetabling. The strongest evidence is the INRC-2010 winner (a two-phase IP decomposition) and IP techniques that improved best-knowns by up to 15%. Day-, employee- and class-based decompositions are the standard neighborhood families. They map almost one-to-one onto CP-SAT "fix most variables, re-solve the rest" loops.

### Cited Findings
- Dorneles, Á.P., de Araújo, O.C.B., Buriol, L.S. (2014), "A fix-and-optimize heuristic for the high school timetabling problem," *Computers & Operations Research* 52:29-38. It combines a MILP model with fix-and-optimize inside a variable neighborhood descent that uses **three decomposition types: class, teacher and day**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305054814001816); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263850578_A_fix-and-optimize_heuristic_for_the_high_school_timetabling_problem)
- Della Croce, F., Salassa, F. (2014), "A variable neighborhood search based matheuristic for nurse rostering problems," *Annals of Operations Research* 218(1):185-199. It is a set of neighborhoods **searched iteratively by a commercial IP solver** under a global time limit, starting from a solution the solver produced on the full IP formulation. It was applied to a real Italian hospital ward and to INRC-2010 instances. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-012-1235-x); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v218y2014i1p185-19910.1007-s10479-012-1235-x.html)
- Valouxis, C., Gogos, C., Goulas, G., Alefragis, P., Housos, E. (2012), "A systematic two phase approach for the nurse rostering problem," *EJOR* 219:425-433. It **won first place in all tracks of INRC-2010**. Phase 1 decides only work/free-day assignments. Phase 2 assigns shift types to working days. IP is combined with local search. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221711011362); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v219y2012i2p425-433.html)
- Santos, H.G., Toffolo, T.A.M., Gomes, R.A.M., Ribas, S. (2016), "Integer programming techniques for the nurse rostering problem," *Annals of OR* 239:225-251. It starts from a compact monolithic formulation and adds **improved cut generation and primal heuristics (MIP-based)**. It **proved optimality for the vast majority of INRC-2010 instances and improved best known solutions by up to 15%**, with strong dual bounds. Code was built on COIN-OR. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-014-1594-6); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v239y2016i1d10.1007_s10479-014-1594-6.html)
- Rahimian, E., Akartunalı, K., Levine, J. (2017), "A hybrid integer programming and variable neighbourhood search algorithm to solve nurse rostering problems," *EJOR* 258(2):411-423. It uses a greedy initial solution, then VND, then IP-based improvement. It was tested on the 24 Curtois & Qu (2014) benchmark instances plus 12 random instances. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v258y2017i2p411-423.html); [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/58402/)
- Relax-and-fix, generically: the integer variables are split into K subsets and K smaller MILPs are solved in sequence. In each one, the current block is integer, later blocks are LP-relaxed, and earlier blocks are fixed. — [Toledo et al., relax-and-fix with fix-and-optimize (J. Heuristics 2015)](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/53600/1/Toledo_etal_JOH_2015_A_relax_and_fix_with_fix_and_optimize_heuristic_applied_to_multi_level.pdf); [arXiv 2107.10738](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.10738)
- Tour scheduling decomposes naturally by day. In a combined shift and days-off IP with aggregate variables and cuts, **fixing the aggregate variables splits the problem into seven daily subproblems** (one per weekday). — [search summary of tour-scheduling literature, e.g., Jarrah/Bard/deSilva "Solving Large-Scale Tour Scheduling Problems," Management Science 40(9) 1994](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.40.9.1124)
- Lü, Z., Hao, J.-K. (2012), "Adaptive neighborhood search for nurse rostering," *EJOR* 218(3):865-876. This pure local search (two neighborhood moves plus adaptive switching among three intensification/diversification strategies) **improved the best known for 12 of 60 INRC-2010 instances and matched the best for 39 others**. The authors note that the top competition solvers were hybrids (ILP, branch-and-price, heuristics). — [PDF](https://leria-info.univ-angers.fr/~jinkao.hao/papers/EJORLuHao2011.pdf); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v218y2012i3p865-876.html)

### Inferences
- The INRC-2010 winning decomposition (Phase 1: work/off days; Phase 2: shift types) is structurally the same as a contact-center "skeleton" stage. The winners closed the gap through iterative re-optimization, not a better one-shot Phase 1. For this scheduler, the analog is to iterate skeleton to breaks to skeleton, not to tune Stage 1 alone.
- Neighborhoods that fit this problem, ranked by expected payoff given that the gap comes from break absorption:
  1. **Day-window neighborhoods with integrated breaks.** Free the skeleton (start times, on/off) for all associates on day d (optionally d-1 and d+1 as well, so rest and weekly constraints are not over-fixed) and solve that sub-model **jointly with that day's break placement** in CP-SAT. Stage 2 is already solved day by day, so a one-day integrated shift-plus-break model is tractable, and it directly attacks the skeleton/break interaction.
  2. **Employee-subset neighborhoods.** Free all days of k associates (random, or those whose shifts overlap the worst post-break deficit intervals) with breaks included for the affected days.
  3. **Shift-start-band neighborhoods.** Free all associates whose start falls in a band around a deficit peak on any day, so starts can shift earlier or later to move break windows.
- Every neighborhood above is **directly implementable on an existing OR-Tools CP-SAT pipeline**. Build one "full" model per sub-problem, fix out-of-neighborhood variables via `model.Add(var == val)` (or bound tightening), pass the incumbent via `AddHint`, and give each sub-solve a short time limit (seconds). This is textbook fix-and-optimize, the method of Helber & Sahling (2010) (background citation: Helber, S., Sahling, F., "A fix-and-optimize approach for the multi-level capacitated lot sizing problem," *Int. J. Production Economics* 123(2):247-256; not retrieved in this session).

### Gaps
- No exact per-instance improvement percentages were retrieved for Della Croce & Salassa, Rahimian et al., or Dorneles et al., because full texts were blocked.
- No paper was found that applies fix-and-optimize specifically to **contact-center tour scheduling with breaks**. The closest verified works are nurse-rostering and timetabling analogs, plus the break-aware decompositions in Q3 and Q5.
- "Fix-and-relax" (the reverse, from the lot-sizing literature) did not turn up any rostering-specific results.

## Q2. ALNS (Ropke & Pisinger) and CP-based LNS for rostering and shift scheduling; destroy/repair operators coupling days-off patterns with break feasibility

### Takeaway
LNS with an exact repair (MIP, CP or branch-and-price) is the dominant large-instance method in recent rostering and driver-scheduling work, including INRC-II (Legrain et al. embed B&P in LNS; Ceschia et al. use SA over large neighborhoods) and bus-driver scheduling with complex break rules (Kletzander/Musliu: CG/B&P repair inside LNS). CP-SAT already runs LNS internally. Adding problem-specific destroy operators driven by post-break deficit is the most direct, low-risk upgrade.

### Cited Findings
- **Integrating column generation and LNS for bus driver scheduling with complex break constraints** (Kletzander, Musliu et al., *JAIR*, 2025; arXiv 2505.02485). The paper studies exact B&P plus an **LNS framework that uses B&P or column generation in the repair phase**. The master problem is set partitioning and the subproblem is a resource-constrained shortest path. The paper reports **the first provably optimal solutions for small instances, improves or proves optimal the best-known solutions for 48 of 50 instances, and achieves an optimality gap below 1% on more than half the instances**. — [JAIR](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/19027); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02485); companion ICAPS paper "Investigating Large Neighbourhood Search for Bus Driver Scheduling" — [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/download/31495/33655/35552)
- Legrain, A., Omer, J., Rosat, S. (2020), "A rotation-based branch-and-price approach for the nurse scheduling problem," *Mathematical Programming Computation* 12:417-450. Columns are **rotations (sequences of consecutive worked days)**. Instances with **up to 120 nurses, 4 shifts and 8 weeks** are handled by **embedding the B&P in a large-neighborhood-search framework**. Open-source code: [GitHub wssuite/NurseScheduler](https://github.com/wssuite/NurseScheduler). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12532-019-00172-4)
- Ceschia, S., Guido, R., Schaerf, A. (2020), "Solving the static INRC-II nurse rostering problem by simulated annealing based on large neighborhoods," *Annals of OR* 288:95-113. Its neighborhoods **change one nurse's assignments, or swap two compatible nurses' assignments, over multiple consecutive days**. It improved on all previous approaches on some datasets and came close to the best on others. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-020-03527-6)
- OR-Tools CP-SAT combines propagation, SAT clause learning, LP relaxations and **LNS in a portfolio of parallel workers**. Perron, Didier & Gay (2023), "The CP-SAT-LP Solver," CP 2023 invited talk, LIPIcs vol. 280. — [DROPS PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol280-cp2023/LIPIcs.CP.2023.3/LIPIcs.CP.2023.3.pdf); a CP-SAT workforce framework (CP-WSP, arXiv 2607.05177) reports first feasible solutions within **5-15 s** with anytime improvement on employee-scheduling benchmarks including INRC-II — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05177)
- Relaxation-induced neighborhoods (RINS), which fix variables where the incumbent and the LP relaxation agree, are a standard MIP LNS operator. — [Danna, Rothberg, Le Pape, "Exploring relaxation induced neighborhoods to improve MILP solutions"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/200035270_Exploring_relaxation_induced_neighborhoods_to_improve_MILP_solutions)
- ALNS for a personnel task scheduling problem with task selection and parallel assignments uses tailored destroy/repair operators whose selection weights adapt to past performance, with a MILP formulation for comparison. — [arXiv 2302.04494](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04494)
- Hybrid tabu search plus branch-and-bound for **multi-activity multi-day shift scheduling** (under- and over-staffing objective, heterogeneous workforce) gives good schedules in short times (*J. Scheduling* 18:207-223, 2015). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-014-0383-z)
- Multi-neighborhood simulated annealing for the multi-activity multi-day shift scheduling problem (*J. Scheduling*, 2026; PATAT 2022 version). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-026-00886-z); [PATAT 2022](https://patatconference.org/patat2022/proceedings/PATAT_2022_paper_02.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Destroy operators suited to the skeleton/break coupling**, all implementable as custom LNS around CP-SAT sub-solves:
  - *Deficit-window destroy.* After running Stage 2, find the intervals with the largest post-break under-coverage. Free every associate whose shift on that day overlaps the window ±k intervals, and let their start times and on/off status change. Repair with the one-day integrated shift-plus-break CP-SAT model.
  - *Break-congestion destroy.* Free associates whose break windows collide, i.e., many breaks forced into the same few intervals by similar start times. The repair can then stagger their starts.
  - *Day-swap / days-off destroy.* For k associates, free their days-off pattern (subject to weekly constraints) together with the start times on the two swapped days. Repair jointly for both days including breaks. This is the only operator family that moves capacity across days, which matters when one weekday is structurally short after breaks.
  - *Related-employee destroy* (Shaw-style relatedness from Ropke & Pisinger (background, unverified): same skill set, same contract, overlapping shifts).
  - *Random destroy* for diversification.
- **Adaptive weights (ALNS)** use the standard reward scheme: new global best, improvement, or accepted. This is cheap to add in Python on top of CP-SAT sub-solves. Acceptance can be greedy or SA-like, following Ceschia et al.
- **Evaluation.** Each LNS iteration should score candidates by the **true post-break objective**, i.e., run the existing near-optimal day-by-day break solver on the modified days only. Use that, not the break-blind Stage-1 objective. This alone removes the proxy mismatch.

### Gaps
- Ropke & Pisinger (2006, *Transportation Science*) is not retrieved here. Its adaptive-weight parameters are from background knowledge only.
- No published ALNS paper specific to **call-center tour scheduling with breaks** was found. The operator suggestions above are inferences.
- No reported quantitative comparison of CP-SAT's built-in LNS vs. custom problem-specific LNS on rostering was found.

## Q3. Rolling horizon / day-by-day decomposition with coupling; Lagrangian relaxation; (logic-based) Benders for shift + break

### Takeaway
The most directly relevant verified result is **Restrepo, Gendron & Rousseau (2018)**. It is Benders decomposition in which the master links daily shifts to weekly tour patterns and **daily subproblems assign activities and breaks**, returning classical plus integer Benders cuts. It **outperforms a prior branch-and-price**. Hooker's LBBD analysis says the single most effective device is to **include a relaxation of the subproblem in the master**. For this scheduler, that means putting an aggregate break-capacity relaxation into the CP-SAT skeleton model and then adding per-day no-good/deficit cuts from the break solver.

### Cited Findings
- Restrepo, M.I., Gendron, B., Rousseau, L.-M. (2018), "Combining Benders decomposition and column generation for multi-activity tour scheduling," *Computers & OR* 93:151-165. The **master problem links daily shifts with tour patterns**, and a set of **daily subproblems assigns work activities and breaks** to shifts. The master is solved by column generation. Subproblems are modeled with **context-free grammars**. **Integer Benders cuts** are added to guarantee convergence under mild conditions. On weekly instances with up to 10 activities, it finds high-quality solutions and **outperforms the previous branch-and-price** for personalized multi-activity tour scheduling. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054818300145); [author page](https://sites.google.com/site/mariarestreporuiz/publications)
- Restrepo et al. also published "A two-stage stochastic programming approach for multi-activity tour scheduling," *EJOR* (2017). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221717304022); and "Branch-and-Price for Personalized Multi-Activity Tour Scheduling" (CIRRELT-2015-03) — [PDF](https://www.cirrelt.ca/documentstravail/cirrelt-2015-03.pdf)
- Logic-based Benders (Hooker): LBBD allows integer or combinatorial subproblems, with problem-specific inference producing cuts. It is **most effective when the planning and scheduling parts are balanced in difficulty**. **The most effective improvement device is including a subproblem relaxation in the master**, followed by strengthening the Benders cuts. — [Hooker, "Logic-based Benders decomposition for planning and scheduling: a computational analysis"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312096713_Logic-based_Benders_decomposition_for_planning_and_scheduling_a_computational_analysis); [Hooker, "Logic-based Benders decomposition for large-scale optimization," arXiv 1910.11944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.11944)
- Rekik, M., Cordeau, J.-F., Soumis, F.: "Using Benders decomposition to implicitly model tour scheduling," *Annals of OR* (2004) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019101.29692.2c); "Implicit shift scheduling with multiple breaks and work stretch duration restrictions," *J. Scheduling* (2010) — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10951-009-0114-z.pdf); "Solution approaches to large shift scheduling problems," *RAIRO-OR* 42(2):229 (2008) — [Numdam](https://www.numdam.org/article/RO_2008__42_2_229_0.pdf). These papers generalize the Bechtold-Jacobs **forward/backward constraints** (implicit break placement) to tour scheduling, **prove their correctness via Benders decomposition**, and give a reformulation that considerably reduces constraint-matrix density. They integrate days-off and shift scheduling in one compact model that handles flexible start times, break lengths and placement. — [search summary](https://publications.polymtl.ca/17713/)
- Kiermaier, F., Frey, M., Bard, J.F. (2020), "The flexible break assignment problem for large tour scheduling problems with an application to airport ground handlers," *J. Scheduling* 23(2):177-209. The paper uses a **two-stage decomposition**: an MIP for tour/shift scheduling (days off plus shifts, minimizing labor and shortage cost), then a second MIP placing breaks. Over **500 instances** were studied. **The most flexible shift and break regulations reduced under-coverage by up to 16.68%** vs. a single lunch break. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-019-00635-5); [TUM preprint](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1552177/document.pdf)
- Hur, Y., Bard, J.F., Frey, M., Kiermaier, F. (2019), "An investigation of shift and break flexibility with real-time break assignments using a rolling horizon approach," *Flexible Services and Manufacturing J.* 31(1). The paper compares **five MIP models**: breaks scheduled in advance vs. sequentially vs. in real time. The **rolling-horizon model gives the greatest advantage under unexpected demand fluctuations**. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10696-018-9305-2)
- Brunner, J.O., Stolletz, R. (2014), "Stabilized branch and price with dynamic parameter updating for discontinuous tour scheduling," *Computers & OR* 44:137-145. It **integrates tour and meal-break scheduling** (breaks within time windows). Reported gaps are **up to 7% with 65 workers under a 1,000-node limit**. — [MADOC](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/35187/)
- Related: "A stochastic optimization approach to shift scheduling with breaks adjustments," *Computers & OR* (2019). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054819300723)

### Inferences
- **A Benders/LBBD design that fits the existing pipeline** (implementable in Python with CP-SAT as master and the existing break solver as subproblem):
  1. **Master (CP-SAT skeleton)** keeps the current model. Add a **break-relaxation in the master**, per Hooker's top recommendation. Use aggregate, implicit break variables per day and shift-start group, following Bechtold-Jacobs/Aykin/Rekik: `b[d, g, t]` = number of breaks taken at interval t by associates of start-group g on day d. Constrain `sum_t b[d,g,t] = n[d,g] * breaks_per_shift` within the break windows, and let net coverage be `sum_g n[d,g]*covers(g,t) - sum_g b[d,g,t]`. The master then optimizes **post-break coverage**, with forward/backward constraints guaranteeing an individual break assignment exists in the single-break-type case (per Rekik et al.). This is the "subproblem relaxation in master." It may close most of the 10-20% gap by itself, because the loss comes from the skeleton ignoring break capacity.
  2. **Subproblem (existing day-by-day break solver)** runs on the master's skeleton and computes the true per-day post-break loss `L_d`.
  3. **Cuts back to the master.**
     - (a) **No-good / combinatorial Benders cut per day.** If day d's start-count vector equals v̂ (or the individual assignment equals x̂), then `loss_d ≥ L_d`. The reified form is `loss_d ≥ L_d − M·(number of changed start-counts on day d)`. In CP-SAT, a Boolean `same_d` is enforced via `OnlyEnforceIf` when all `n[d,g] == v̂[d,g]`, together with `loss_d ≥ L_d` under `same_d`.
     - (b) **Capacity (window) cuts.** For any interval window W where Stage 2 found deficit, derive a valid inequality: the sum of on-floor agent-intervals in W minus the minimum number of breaks that *must* fall in W (breaks whose windows lie entirely inside W) must be at least the target. Only the break-feasibility aspect is exact. These are "Hall-type" cuts like the forward/backward constraints and are generated lazily only for violated windows.
  4. Iterate. Because the break solver is fast (day by day), each Benders iteration costs one CP-SAT master re-solve (warm-started with hints) plus seven cheap daily solves.
- The problem is well suited to LBBD because the subproblem separates by day and is already near-optimal and fast. That matches the "balanced difficulty" condition only partially: the master is harder. This argues for step 1 (relaxation in master) doing most of the work and cuts serving as a correction.
- **Lagrangian relaxation of coupling constraints** (weekly hours, days-off patterns, rest between days) would split the weekly skeleton into daily problems solvable jointly with breaks. It fits CP-SAT less naturally than LNS, because multipliers would have to be updated outside the solver, and no verified rostering-with-breaks Lagrangian result was retrieved.

### Gaps
- No quantitative numbers (e.g., % improvement over B&P, CPU times) for Restrepo et al. (2018) were retrievable; only the qualitative "outperforms" claim.
- No verified paper uses **logic-based Benders with a CP break subproblem specifically**. Restrepo et al. use grammar-based MIP subproblems. Hooker's LBBD work covers planning/scheduling in general.
- No Lagrangian-relaxation paper for tour scheduling with breaks was retrieved in this session.

## Q4. Column generation / branch-and-price heuristics (rounding, diving, price-and-branch, restricted master) for set-covering tour models; step objectives

### Takeaway
CG-based heuristics give the best-known results on the largest nurse-rostering benchmarks: Strandmark et al. 2020, Burke & Curtois 2014, and Legrain et al. 2020 inside LNS. Grammar/implicit models (Côté et al.) handle billions of implicit shifts. For a CP-SAT-based Python shop, however, CG is a heavy build. Step (threshold) objectives weaken LP relaxations, and no retrieved paper addresses CG with step objectives directly.

### Cited Findings
- Strandmark, P., Qu, Y., Curtois, T. (2020), "First-order linear programming in a column generation-based heuristic approach to the nurse rostering problem," *Computers & OR* 120:104945. It is an **incomplete CG search** (no valid lower bounds) that uses a first-order LP method. It is **effective on large instances and produced best-known solutions on benchmark instances**, and needed several innovations to handle the largest instances in acceptable time. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054820300629); [Northumbria](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/first-order-linear-programming-in-a-column-generation-based-heuri/)
- Burke, E.K., Curtois, T. (2014), "New approaches to nurse rostering benchmark instances," *EJOR* 237(1):71-81. It compares a branch-and-price with an ejection-chain VDS. **B&P outperformed VDS especially on medium and long instances.** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221714000605); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeeejores/v_3a237_3ay_3a2014_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a71-81.htm)
- Côté, M.-C., Gendron, B., Rousseau, L.-M. (2011), "Grammar-based integer programming models for multiactivity shift scheduling," *Management Science* 57(1):151-163. Its implicit context-free-grammar formulation allows the same shift set as Dantzig's set covering model. CPLEX branch-and-bound on the implicit model gives **optimal solutions in relatively short times for environments with up to 2.97 billion explicit shifts**. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1100.1264)
- Legrain et al. (2020) embed rotation-based B&P in LNS for up to 120 nurses × 8 weeks (see Q2). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12532-019-00172-4). A dedicated pricing algorithm for a large family of nurse-scheduling problems with B&P appears in *INFORMS JoC* (2024/25). — [INFORMS JoC](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2023.0019)
- Brunner & Stolletz (2014): stabilized B&P for tour plus break scheduling; gaps up to 7% at 65 workers with 1,000 nodes (see Q3). — [MADOC](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/35187/)
- Rastgar-Amini, F., Contardo, C., Desaulniers, G., et al. (2025), "Learning to enumerate shifts for large-scale flexible personnel scheduling problems," *J. Scheduling*. Deep-learning models predict time points where promising shifts start or end, filtering the shift set, because **commercial solvers need very long times on practical sizes and rely on enumeration heuristics**. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-025-00844-1)
- Call-center service level as a non-linear, non-concave staffing curve. Piecewise-linear approximations of the TSF curve enable joint staffing and scheduling with a **global** service-level requirement. Cutting-plane methods (Atlason/Epelman/Henderson with simulation; Ingolfsson & Cabral with queueing models) linearize service-level constraints iteratively. — [Atlason et al., "Call center staffing with simulation and cutting plane methods," Annals of OR 127 (2004)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019095.91642.bb); [Atlason, Epelman, Henderson, Management Science 54 (2008)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1070.0774); [Robbins, "Complexity and flexibility in call center scheduling models"](https://myweb.ecu.edu/robbinst/PDFs/IJBSS%2017523-TR.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Step/threshold objectives** (count intervals with coverage ≥ target) have very weak LP relaxations. A fractional solution can "almost" meet many thresholds. This hurts CG and price-and-branch (dual prices are uninformative), while CP-SAT's clause learning handles reified thresholds natively. Two consequences:
  - For any LP/CG-based component, replace the step with a **piecewise-linear concave surrogate** (e.g., shortfall penalty with steep first-unit cost) to get useful duals. The Atlason/Ingolfsson cutting-plane line applies the same idea to service-level curves.
  - For CP-SAT, keep the step objective but add the **linear shortfall** as a secondary or lexicographic term. It gives the search gradient information and has been observed (background, unverified) to speed CP-SAT convergence on threshold objectives.
- Building a full CG/B&P in Python is a substantial project. A pragmatic "restricted master" analog on CP-SAT is to generate a pool of good weekly tours per associate from LNS incumbents, then solve a set-partitioning CP-SAT/MIP over that pool (a "tour-pool recombination" matheuristic). This captures much of price-and-branch's benefit without pricing.

### Gaps
- No retrieved paper evaluates CG or price-and-branch **with step (threshold) coverage objectives** specifically.
- No quantitative diving/rounding comparison (e.g., price-and-branch vs. full B&P gap) was retrievable for tour scheduling.

## Q5. Surrogate / proxy objectives for Stage-1 skeletons that anticipate breaks; two-stage shift-then-break with feedback

### Takeaway
There is direct precedent for exactly this situation. **Akkermans, Post & Uetz (2021)** create shifts in phase 1 "while heuristically taking breaks into account," assign breaks in phase 2, and **iterate until no improvement**. They beat the previous best method (the Di Gaspero/Musliu hybrid) on all benchmark instances. **Dahmen et al. (2020)** use an aggregated first stage whose output guides personalized scheduling. Implicit break models (Bechtold-Jacobs / Rekik et al.) are the principled "break absorption capacity" surrogate.

### Cited Findings
- Akkermans, A., Post, G., Uetz, M. (2021), "Solving the shift and break design problem using integer linear programming," *Annals of OR* 302(2):341-362 (PATAT 2018 version available). It is a two-phase ILP. **Phase 1 creates shifts while heuristically taking breaks into account. Phase 2 assigns breaks to each shift occurrence one by one, and the process repeats until no improvement.** On random plus real-life benchmarks it **beats the current best known method** and **obtains better results on all instances** compared with the best in the literature. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03487-6); [UTwente](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/solving-the-shift-and-break-design-problem-using-integer-linear-p/); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v302y2021i2d10.1007_s10479-019-03487-6.html); Akkermans MSc thesis — [UTwente essay](https://essay.utwente.nl/74147/1/Akkermans_MA_EEMCS.pdf)
- Di Gaspero, L., Gärtner, J., Musliu, N., Schaerf, A., Schafhauser, W., Slany, W. (2010), "A hybrid LS-CP solver for the shifts and breaks design problem," Hybrid Metaheuristics (LNCS), and the book chapter "Automated shift design and break scheduling" (2013, Springer, in *Automated Scheduling and Planning*). Shift design is solved **together with** break scheduling by combining **local search with constraint programming** (and min-cost max-flow). The hybrid improved results on benchmarks. — [Springer chapter 2010](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-16054-7_4); [Springer chapter 2013](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-39304-4_5)
- Widl, M., Musliu, N. (2014), "The break scheduling problem: complexity results and practical algorithms," *Memetic Computing*. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12293-014-0131-0)
- Dahmen, S., Rekik, M., Soumis, F., Desaulniers, G. (2020), "A two-stage solution approach for personalized multi-department multi-day shift scheduling," *EJOR* 280(3):1051-1063. **Stage 1 solves an approximate, smaller aggregated problem** (producing approximate transfers). **Stage 2 builds personalized schedules** from Stage-1 information. The objective is under- and over-coverage plus transfer and labor costs. An exhaustive study shows efficiency in quality and time. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221719306472); [PolyPublie](https://publications.polymtl.ca/44284/); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v280y2020i3p1051-1063.html)
- Dahmen, S., Rekik, M., Soumis, F. (2018), "An implicit model for multi-activity shift scheduling problems," *J. Scheduling*. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-017-0544-y)
- Rekik, Cordeau & Soumis: implicit forward/backward constraints that model **break placement flexibility implicitly** within shift and tour models, with correctness shown via Benders (see Q3). — [Springer JoS 2010](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10951-009-0114-z.pdf)
- Kiermaier, Frey & Bard (2020): two-stage tour-then-break MIP decomposition. Break flexibility changes under-coverage by up to 16.68% depending on demand shape (see Q3). This shows how much of coverage depends on break design. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-019-00635-5)
- Optimal implicit models for meal-break and start-time flexibility in continuous tour scheduling (*Management Science* 46(12), 2000, Jacobs/Brusco line of work). — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.46.12.1630.12074)
- Simulation/queueing cutting-plane feedback (Atlason et al.) is the call-center precedent for a "master picks shifts, evaluator returns cuts" loop. — [Annals of OR](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:ANOR.0000019095.91642.bb)

### Inferences
- **Concrete surrogate objectives for the Stage-1 CP-SAT skeleton**, from cheapest to strongest:
  1. **Break-inflated demand.** Replace target `D_t` with `D_t + expected breaks-in-progress at t`. This is crude, because the break load depends on the skeleton itself.
  2. **Break absorption capacity / headroom.** For each interval t, require or reward `onfloor_t − D_t ≥ h_t`, where `h_t` is the number of breaks that must be taken near t by shifts covering it. Endogenize `h_t` from the shift-start variables, e.g. `h_t ≥ sum_g n[d,g] · P(break of group g falls in t)`, or better the window-aggregated version: for window W, `sum_{t∈W} (onfloor_t − D_t) ≥ breaks forced into W by starts in the skeleton`.
  3. **Implicit break variables** (Bechtold-Jacobs/Aykin/Rekik) in the skeleton model, so Stage 1 optimizes net-of-breaks coverage exactly in aggregate (no individual break assignment). This is the "subproblem relaxation in master" of Q3. It is directly implementable in CP-SAT with integer count variables per (day, start-group, break-slot), and it keeps the per-day individual break placement in Stage 2.
  4. **Feedback cuts** from Stage 2 (Q3 no-good/window cuts) plus iteration (Akkermans-style), until no improvement.
- The user's symptom (100% pre-break coverage but 10-20% post-break loss) is exactly what options 2-3 target. Pre-break coverage at 100% with no headroom means breaks necessarily create deficits wherever break windows cluster. Staggering starts to spread break windows is the lever, and only a Stage 1 that "sees" breaks will stagger.

### Gaps
- The exact phase-1 break heuristic in Akkermans et al. (how breaks are "heuristically taken into account") and its numeric improvement margins could not be read. Full text was blocked (PATAT 2018 paper16 and utwente preprint).
- No paper was found that defines a named "break absorption capacity" metric. The surrogate designs above are inferences built on implicit-break modeling.

## Q6. Nurse Rostering Competition (INRC-I / INRC-II) and benchmark winners: techniques and why

### Takeaway
**INRC-I (2010) was won by a two-phase IP decomposition** (Valouxis et al.: days-on/off first, then shift types, plus local search). **INRC-II was won by a direct MILP on a state-expanded network-flow formulation** (Römer & Mellouli). Strong later methods are MIP-heavy hybrids (IP techniques with up to 15% improvements, B&P-in-LNS, CG heuristics) or large-neighborhood SA. Winners won through strong formulations combined with decomposition or LNS, not pure metaheuristics.

### Cited Findings
- INRC-2010 winner: Valouxis et al. (EJOR 2012), first in all tracks. Two phases: (1) working vs. free day assignments; (2) shift types on working days. IP plus local search. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221711011362); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v219y2012i2p425-433.html)
- INRC-II rules: multi-stage (week-by-week) nurse rostering over 4 or 8 weeks. Each week is solved in sequence with history carried forward (a rolling horizon by design). Adjudication followed INRC-I / ITC-2007 procedures. — [arXiv 1501.04177](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.04177); [Ceschia, Dang, De Causmaecker, Haspeslagh, Schaerf, "The Second International Nurse Rostering Competition," Annals of OR 274 (2019)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v274y2019i1d10.1007_s10479-018-2816-0.html)
- INRC-II winner: **Römer & Mellouli (2016)**, a **direct MILP based on state-expanded (multi-commodity) network flows with anticipation** of future weeks. — [search summary citing Mischek & Musliu, "Integer programming model extensions for a multi-stage nurse rostering problem," Annals of OR (2019)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-017-2623-z); [PMC full text of Mischek & Musliu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6394597/)
- The state-expanded network idea was extended to multi-activity shift scheduling: "Block-based state-expanded network models for multi-activity shift scheduling," *J. Scheduling* (2023) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-023-00789-3); "Learning to reduce state-expanded networks for multi-activity shift scheduling" (CPAIOR 2021) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-78230-6_24)
- Post-competition INRC-II: Legrain et al. B&P-in-LNS (open source, and the Polytechnique team's competition code is on GitHub) — [GitHub DynamicNurseScheduler](https://github.com/jiaodaxiaozi/DynamicNurseScheduler); Ceschia et al. SA with large neighborhoods improves on all previous methods on some datasets — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-020-03527-6)
- INRC-I post-competition: Santos et al. IP techniques proved most instances optimal and improved best-knowns by up to 15% — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-014-1594-6). Lü & Hao ANS improved 12 and matched 39 of 60 — [PDF](https://leria-info.univ-angers.fr/~jinkao.hao/papers/EJORLuHao2011.pdf)
- Curtois & Qu (2014) benchmark (24 instances): Burke & Curtois B&P beats ejection-chain VDS on medium and long instances — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221714000605). Strandmark et al.'s CG heuristic reports best-knowns on large instances — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054820300629).

### Inferences
- The recurring "why" behind the winners: (i) a **strong formulation** of sequence and pattern constraints (network flow over states, or rotations/columns), which gives tight relaxations; (ii) **decomposition or LNS** to keep sub-MIPs small; (iii) **local search** for final polishing. For a CP-SAT user, the analog of (i) is modeling weekly patterns with automaton/regular constraints (CP-SAT `AddAutomaton`) or precomputed allowed day-patterns via `AddAllowedAssignments`. These are the CP equivalents of state-expanded networks.
- INRC-II's week-by-week format with history is itself a rolling horizon. Römer & Mellouli's **anticipation** of future stages is a surrogate-objective technique conceptually parallel to "anticipating breaks" in Stage 1.

### Gaps
- The official INRC-II second- and third-place teams and methods could not be verified (the competition site mobiz.vives.be was blocked). Background, unverified: Legrain/Omer/Rosat placed among the finalists.
- No shift-scheduling-with-breaks benchmark competition equivalent to INRC was found. The Musliu shift/break design benchmarks (used by Di Gaspero et al. and Akkermans et al.) are the closest.

## Q7. Anytime behavior under 15-60 minute budgets

### Takeaway
Under 15-60 minute budgets, the literature favors **LNS/matheuristics with exact sub-solvers** (B&P or MIP repair, IP-based VNS, SA over large neighborhoods) over monolithic MIP. Exact B&P rarely closes gaps at scale (e.g., 7% gaps at 65 workers under node limits). CP-SAT gives feasible solutions within seconds and improves anytime. A custom, problem-aware LNS layered on CP-SAT is the best-supported choice.

### Cited Findings
- CP-SAT: portfolio including LNS workers. In a workforce-scheduling framework it gives first feasible solutions in **5-15 s** with anytime incumbent improvement. — [arXiv 2607.05177](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05177); [Perron et al. CP 2023](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CP.2023.3)
- Della Croce & Salassa: IP-solver-driven VNS under a **global time limit**, starting from a monolithic solver incumbent. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-012-1235-x)
- Strandmark et al.: incomplete CG search that "quickly produces good solutions" on large instances, trading away bounds. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054820300629)
- Brunner & Stolletz: exact stabilized B&P for tour plus breaks reaches **up to 7% gap** at 65 workers with a 1,000-node limit, which shows the limits of exact methods at moderate size. — [MADOC](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/35187/)
- Commercial solvers need very long times at practical sizes of flexible personnel scheduling, which motivates learned shift-set reduction. — [Rastgar-Amini et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-025-00844-1)
- Hybrid tabu plus B&B for multi-activity multi-day shift scheduling: "good schedules in relatively short computing times." — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10951-014-0383-z)
- Kletzander/Musliu: LNS with CG/B&P repair improved or proved optimal 48/50 best-knowns for bus-driver scheduling with complex breaks. — [JAIR](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/19027)

### Inferences
- **Recommended anytime pipeline for a 15-60 min budget on the existing Python CP-SAT stack:**
  1. (0-2 min) Solve the **break-aware skeleton** (implicit aggregate break variables in the master, Q3/Q5) with CP-SAT and multiple workers, and hint from the current skeleton.
  2. (2-50 min) **Custom ALNS loop.** Destroy with deficit-window, break-congestion, day-swap and employee-subset operators (Q2). Repair with a one- or two-day integrated shift-plus-break CP-SAT sub-model (5-30 s limit, hinted). Evaluate with the true Stage-2 break solver on the touched days. Update operator weights adaptively.
  3. Every N iterations, add **Benders-style no-good/window cuts** to the master (Q3) and optionally re-solve the master briefly for a global restructure.
  4. (Last minutes) Final day-by-day break optimization (existing Stage 2).
- The first two items are low-risk and most directly target the measured gap. Full CG/B&P is not recommended for the budget and stack unless the team is prepared for a large engineering investment.
- Expected impact: no retrieved paper reports gains on an identical contact-center setting. The closest analogs are Akkermans et al. (iterative break-aware two-phase beats the prior best on all instances) and Kiermaier et al. (break/shift flexibility changes under-coverage by up to 16.68%). Recovering a meaningful share of the 10-20% post-break loss is plausible but unquantified.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head study was found that compares monolithic MIP/CP vs. LNS vs. Benders under a fixed 15/30/60-minute budget on the same shift-plus-break instances.
- No published CP-SAT-specific results on large tour-plus-break scheduling were found. CP-WSP is an employee-scheduling framework, and its INRC-II numbers could not be read.
