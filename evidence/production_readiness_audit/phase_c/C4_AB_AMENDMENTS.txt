Phase C4 impact A/B - AMENDMENT to C4_AB_RULE.txt (written after the rule was pushed
in 58acabf, and before any A/B run started)
=====================================================================================

A1  Seeds: 2 per case (9000, 9001) instead of 3, at the business's request, to cut
    the wall-clock time from about 14 h to about 9 h (18 new runs instead of 27; AEIT
    INTERVAL still reuses the C3 DEFAULT runs of the same seeds).

A2  A third seed (9002) is run for a case, both arms, when any of these holds after
    its two seeds:
      - the two paired req_covered deltas have opposite signs (one positive, one
        negative), so rule B's direction is not settled for that case;
      - the two seeds give different prices (one MINOR, one MAJOR, applying rule
        C's thresholds to each seed's delta);
      - a VOLUME run fails rule A. A rule-A failure is still a defect: it is
        root-caused whatever the third seed shows.
    The case is then scored on its three seeds.

Nothing else changes: arms, cases, engine, command, measures, rules A, B and C, and
the verdicts are as written in C4_AB_RULE.txt. Two seeds show direction and size,
not a confidence interval.

A3  (written 2026-10-04 11:30 UTC, after the VOICE and CHAT seed-9000 pairs and before
    any other result.) The A/B's frozen engine (e211adeb) carries audit F-35: the
    shift-consistency polish replaced the measured schedule but never the published one,
    and when it moved a shift by an hour the audit and the published workbook disagreed
    and parity failed (VOICE INTERVAL 9000, exit 4). The release engine fixes F-35 by
    publishing, and measuring, exactly the schedule the frozen engine publishes, so every
    A/B workbook is the workbook the release engine would publish. Rule A is therefore
    applied as follows: a VOLUME run whose only rule-A failure is metric parity, where
    (a) its polish record shows at least one move, and (b) the engine's RECOMMENDED_FINAL
    export metrics (the published workbook) equal the validator's value on every mismatching
    field the export records, is attributed to F-35,
    reported as such, and not counted as a Volume Weighted defect. Every other rule-A
    check (validator hard failures, clean-room, language gaps, empty quarters, crashes)
    applies unchanged, and the measures of rules B and C come from the validator, which
    reads the published workbook, so they are unaffected.

A4  (written 2026-10-04 18:58 UTC, after GDI VOLUME 9001 ended FAIL_METRIC_PARITY; a
    clarification of how A3 is applied, not a new exemption.) On a run whose parity
    failure A3 attributes to F-35, the validator's hard_fail_count includes the
    METRIC_PARITY_MISMATCH record itself, and the runner never reaches the clean-room
    gate (it stops at the parity failure). So for such a run: the hard-failure check
    counts every validator failure except METRIC_PARITY_MISMATCH, and the clean-room
    checker is run by the scorer on the published workbook (tools/clean_room_check.py,
    rule violations only; its comparison with the engine audit is the F-35 mismatch and
    is not used). Both must still be zero. Applies equally to every arm and every run.
