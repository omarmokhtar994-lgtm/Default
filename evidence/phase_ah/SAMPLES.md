## The sample (`samples/ah01_hours_needed.png`, day look `_day.png`; export `samples/ah02_export.xlsx`)

Real figures from the review week, with people marked sick on the RTA on Thursday (3), Friday (6) and Saturday (7).

- **Colour rule, from the week's target** (90% here; each week can have its own): 100% or more teal; from the target
  to 99% amber; below the target red. Today the week grid turns amber at a fixed 90% whatever the week's target is;
  the same rule would go to the RTA's interval board, its day strip and Overview.
- **Hours needed** on the Weeks page: one row per interval, a column per day; each cell gives the hours needed to
  reach 100% (to the quarter hour) and the share on the floor, and says what fixes it: Day off, OT, or both. Rows
  where every day is at 100% or more fold into one line. Totals: hours needed, intervals below the target, intervals
  at the target but under 100%.
- **Filter:** all short intervals, overtime needed, or a day off to cancel.
- **As recorded on the RTA** (the default) or **as planned**: recorded counts what the RTA keeps (sick and absences,
  overtime, called-in days, aux, shrinkage); planned is the schedule alone.
- **What to do**, per day: cancel a day off when one person covers at least 6 short intervals of their shift (the
  rule in use since Phase AF), naming up to 5 people off that day, those who keep every rule first; then overtime
  for what is left, naming who can stay on or come in early within the rest rules. Friday: overtime is enough;
  Saturday: one day off to cancel (10:00 to 19:00), then overtime at 11:00, 16:00, 18:00 and 21:00.
- **Download this table:** the same table and colours in Excel, with the suggestions on a second sheet.

## Rescue the day and Fix breaks: checked

Method: both run on every day of two fixture weeks (the review week; the channel week with its Arabic rule and
Phone, Chat and Email plans), as they are and with 6 to 14 people marked sick, for another day and at 13:07 today.
Each proposal was applied through the RTA's own figures and checked against what it promises.

- **Fix breaks** (the autopilot): never left the tightest interval tighter, never added hours short, never lowered
  the intervals at the target, never opened a language gap or a channel warning, kept every break inside its shift,
  and never moved a break into the past or one already started. No defect found.
- **Rescue the day:** never fewer intervals at the target, never more than 3 given up, never an interval below 50%,
  no new language gap or channel warning, breaks inside their shifts, nothing in the past. No defect found.
- **They aim at different things**, which the numbers show (review week, Saturday, 6 people sick, target 90%):
  - before: 19 intervals at the target, the tightest 1.75 hours short, 6.25 hours short in all;
  - Fix breaks: 20 at the target, the tightest 1.25 hours short, 5.25 hours short in all;
  - Rescue: 23 at the target, the tightest still 1.75 hours short, 5.34 hours short in all.
  Fix breaks lifts the weakest interval; Rescue gets the most intervals to the target.
- On days short by a whole person or more in most intervals, both say there is nothing to gain: moving 15-minute
  breaks cannot add a person. That is when the new table's overtime and days off apply.
